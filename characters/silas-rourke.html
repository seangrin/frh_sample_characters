Silas Rourke

Ruined-but-useful ex-detective whose survival has started to look like evidence of something much older trying to remain disguised as a man with a badge and a drinking problem.

Points: 375 Age: 42 Public Read: Worn, suspicious, rail-adjacent ex-detective with a disgraced case on his record and a constitution that keeps defying arithmetic Core Role: Psychic residue investigator, precognitive danger reader, and field-hardened noir operator whose body is part of the mystery

Powers

  • ESP 6 [18] and Psi Sense 1 [1]: Silas reads residue - not just object history but spatial and personal traces, danger approach, and psychic signatures that precede visible threat. His psionic lane is investigative rather than dramatic.
  • Psychometry-15 (36-year reach): residue-based, strongest on objects handled within hours. A crime scene object, a handled weapon, or a personal belonging gives him direct investigative access to recent events.
  • Precognition-15: also residue-based and situational. Not reliable prediction - situational flash-sense of imminent danger or outcome probability, strongest when he is already close to the event.
  • Supernatural constitution: Regeneration (1 HT/hr), Disease Resistance, Poison Resistance, Hard to Kill 4, DR 2, Night Vision, Move 9. His survival is ugly in the FRH sense - it has been too consistent for too long to look like luck anymore.
  • IQ 18 [125]: one of the highest raw intelligence scores in the library, powering an extremely capable Detect Lies-18 and broad investigative skill set at well above average.

Required Secret Power

Psychic and Supernatural Constitution (grouped):

  • ESP 6 [18] and Psi Sense 1 [1] - psionic detection layer
  • Precognition-15 and Psychometry-15 - the operational psi skills
  • Regeneration (1 HT/hr) [25] - recovery rate beyond normal human range
  • Hard to Kill 4 [20], Disease-Resistant [5], Resistant to Poison [5], DR 2 [6], Night Vision [10] - the accumulated evidence that something deeper than luck is keeping him alive

Secret (Psychic and Supernatural Constitution) [-20]: the full picture - psi abilities plus unusual resilience - would draw occult groups, secret societies, and institutions that want to study, recruit, or classify him. Even Silas may not want to know what exactly is causing it.

Required Secret Society

Patron (Freemasons) (9 or less) [10].

Silas's non-FRH hidden-order lane is the Freemasons. FRH does not count toward this requirement. His Savoir-Faire (Masonic Lodge)-17 reflects real operational familiarity with Masonic networks and their social geography.

Secret (Patron (Freemasons)) [-5].

His affiliation is concealed. The Masonic connection gives him access to a specific type of institutional network - police-adjacent, fraternal, and respectable in ways that can be leveraged or hidden depending on the case.

How Silas Uses His Powers

  • His Psychometry gives him access to recent object history - a handled weapon, a personal belonging, a crime scene artifact can produce a flash of residue that no physical evidence analysis can match. Strongest on objects touched within hours.
  • His Precognition functions as a danger-proximity warning and outcome-probability flash rather than reliable narrative prediction. He does not see the future - he gets an ugly sense that something specific is wrong before anyone else knows it.
  • His Detect Lies-18 backed by IQ 18 makes him one of the most capable interview and interrogation operators in the library. He notices what witnesses are hiding before they have finished the sentence.
  • His contact network (bartender/information broker, honest patrolman, major newspaper reporter, mob fixer, rail dispatcher) gives him city-level and rail-adjacent intelligence access that reflects decades of dirty, effective field work.
  • His Alcoholism is a genuine operating constraint, not flavor text. It creates real risk in high-stakes sequences and makes him a character whose capability is always offset by active liability.

Why He Works

Silas proves that a psychic investigator hits hardest when the psi lane is residue-based and investigative rather than flashy. The question of what keeps him alive - bloodline, bargain, or inheritance - is itself a live investigative thread that the campaign can open if it chooses to.

Character Overview

Silas Rourke should by rights be dead, or at least ordinary in the sort of bitter, middle-aged way that ex-detectives often are when the city has finished chewing on them. Instead he is still alive, still functional, and suspicious in both respects. He comes out of police work, rail-adjacent investigations, ugly cases, and the sort of practical life that teaches a man to read people, scenes, and lies before it teaches him anything noble. At some point one railway case went bad enough to disgrace him, and that disgrace still hangs on the character like smoke on a coat. The useful thing is that disgrace did not end his career so much as push him deeper into the kinds of work and the kinds of people respectable institutions would rather not acknowledge publicly.

What makes Silas unnerving is not just that he has psychic edges. It is that his survival itself feels wrong. He reads residue, senses danger, and comes through poisons, beatings, and disasters in ways that no longer look lucky once a man has seen them recur often enough. That does not make him reassuring. It makes him ugly in the useful FRH sense: a person whose body says something ominous about cost, inheritance, or unfinished business. He is the sort of man occult groups would want to study, secret societies would want to recruit, and sensible institutions would prefer to classify as a problem before deciding whether he is also an asset.

Silas remains grounded because the noir veteran never quite leaves the occult heavy hitter. He still knows bars, railway offices, underpaid officials, bad police logic, and the difference between a frightened witness and a lying one. He is not above a drink, not above resentment, and not above using fear as a tool if the case needs it. But the deeper hook in him is that his powers may not be entirely his. The question of what he really is, or whose bloodline or bargain still speaks through him, is dangerous enough that even Silas may not want the answer very badly.

For a new player, Silas should feel like a ruined-but-useful investigator whose survival is part of the mystery. He is worn, suspicious, and too hard to put down - the kind of man who can still work a case while making everyone around him quietly wonder whether he is merely unlucky in an unusual way or evidence of something much older trying to remain disguised as a detective.
