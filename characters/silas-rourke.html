Description

Silas Rourke should by rights be dead, or at least ordinary in the sort of bitter, middle-aged way that ex-detectives often are when the city has finished chewing on them. Instead he is still alive, still functional, and suspicious in both respects. He comes out of police work, rail-adjacent investigations, ugly cases, and the sort of practical life that teaches a man to read people, scenes, and lies before it teaches him anything noble. At some point one railway case went bad enough to disgrace him, and that disgrace still hangs on the character like smoke on a coat. The useful thing is that disgrace did not end his career so much as push him deeper into the kinds of work and the kinds of people respectable institutions would rather not acknowledge publicly.

What makes Silas unnerving is not just that he has psychic edges. It is that his survival itself feels wrong. He reads residue, senses danger, and comes through poisons, beatings, and disasters in ways that no longer look lucky once a man has seen them recur often enough. That does not make him reassuring. It makes him ugly in the useful FRH sense: a person whose body says something ominous about cost, inheritance, or unfinished business. He is the sort of man occult groups would want to study, secret societies would want to recruit, and sensible institutions would prefer to classify as a problem before deciding whether he is also an asset.

Silas remains grounded because the noir veteran never quite leaves the occult heavy hitter. He still knows bars, railway offices, underpaid officials, bad police logic, and the difference between a frightened witness and a lying one. He is not above a drink, not above resentment, and not above using fear as a tool if the case needs it. But the deeper hook in him is that his powers may not be entirely his. The question of what he really is, or whose bloodline or bargain still speaks through him, is dangerous enough that even Silas may not want the answer very badly.

For a new player, Silas should feel like a ruined-but-useful investigator whose survival is part of the mystery. He is worn, suspicious, and too hard to put down, the kind of man who can still work a case while making everyone around him quietly wonder whether he is merely unlucky in an unusual way or evidence of something much older trying to remain disguised as a detective.
