Powers

  • Telepathy 10: Felix reads rooms through pressure, projection, desire, and emotional leakage rather than flashy overt mind-attack.
  • Interpersonal penetration: he catches what people are really prepared to confess, hide, or project onto him.
  • Social danger: his power is strongest where shame, appetite, vice, class performance, and fear are already packed together too tightly.

The current sheet uses a generic label rather than a more colorful named order. For now the page preserves that wording and treats it as Felix's hidden discipline-and-recovery society rather than silently inventing a new proper name.

How Felix Uses His Powers

  • He keeps rooms from going bad by reading appetite, fear, humiliation, and dangerous attention before violence starts.
  • He protects performers, vulnerable clients, and useful witnesses by spotting when the social air has turned predatory.
  • He is strongest in clubs, cabarets, vice rooms, private parties, discreet blackmail situations, and any scene where desire and disgrace are only one mistake apart.
  • His power does not make him invulnerable. It exposes him to everyone else, which is why discipline and addiction sit so close together in his story.

Character Overview

Felix Marot learned young that nightlife is not an escape from the real world but one of the places where it tells the truth most quickly. In rooms built out of music, money, vice, danger, and performance, people become legible if you know how to watch them. Felix made himself indispensable in those rooms. He became a fixer, floor operator, scandal-stopper, comforter, threat-assessor, and quiet broker between performers, discreet patrons, mob men, lonely women, frightened boys, and the sort of men who thought expensive cigarettes counted as charm. His gift for surviving came first from memory, wit, style, and perfect room-reading. Even without anything supernatural, he would have been dangerous.

The telepathic gift made him stranger rather than simpler. Felix does not possess the clean power fantasy of a man who merely reads minds. What he lives with is more intimate and more exhausting. Rooms press on him. Other people tell him, silently and constantly, what version of Felix they are prepared to see. Secrets arrive before he has consented to hold them. Desire, fear, projection, and shame try to colonize his self-concept if he is tired enough, drunk enough, or lonely enough to let them. That is why addiction and discipline sit so close together in him. Vice dulls the flood; training might save him. He knows that. He also suspects that surrendering fully to psychic discipline might cost him some hard-won, improvised, beautifully unstable version of himself that he is not yet ready to lose.

That tension is why Felix works so well in FRH. He is not a decorative underworld queer, not a decadent cliche, and not a broad telepathic answer machine. He is a dignity-first nightlife operator whose power has become a metaphysical extension of the social work he already did. He manages disaster before it becomes blood. He hears what people never meant to confess. He carries one face into a room and leaves with another if survival demands it. The hidden psi society offers him a recovery path, a temptation, perhaps even a substitute religion. He has not decided yet whether it would heal him or erase him.

For a new player, Felix should feel elegant, useful, unstable, and fully grown. He is the man who can keep a room from collapsing, make vice serve investigation, and talk to people on the edge of disgrace without pretending innocence. His power does not make him stronger than other people so much as more exposed to them, and that is why he remains so compelling and so dangerous.
