Evelyn Alder

Rural witch and land mediator who turns weather, beasts, old rites, and wild places into investigative terrain that urban investigators cannot reach alone.

Powers

  • Magery 2 with natural spellcasting: Evelyn's magic is old-ways in character - beast speech, shapeshifting, weather, plant and earth manipulation, spirit sensing, and healing. It is not arcane in the academic sense. It is continuous with land, creature, and older rite.
  • Great Shapeshifting-21: her most dramatic power. She can become an animal with full physiological capability, not merely cosmetic change.
  • Beast-Kin, Animal Empathy, Plant Empathy, Spirit Empathy, Speak with Animals: these advantages extend her perceptual range into ecological and spiritual registers no other investigator covers.
  • Danger Sense and Absolute Direction: she does not get lost, and she does not miss warnings that arrive through field, wind, or behavioral change rather than language.

Required Secret Society

The old-ways practitioner network.

Unusual Background (Initiate of surviving old-ways rites) [10] establishes Evelyn's membership in a distributed network of practitioners who recognize each other through shared rites, hospitality obligations, and older knowledge. Claim to Hospitality [5] is the practical expression of that network's mutual recognition.

Secret (Old-ways shapeshifter and witch) [-20] covers both the society membership and the power lane simultaneously - a dual-purpose Secret that conceals the nature of her abilities and the community that produced them. A formal Patron advantage is not required. FRH does not count toward this requirement.

How Evelyn Uses Her Powers

  • She expands the party's clue surface into terrain that has no paperwork: fields, tree lines, water, ridge weather, animal behavior, old rites at place boundaries, and spiritual contamination that predates the case's visible timeline.
  • Great Shapeshifting lets her access areas where human form is a liability - nocturnal surveillance, river crossing, dense cover entry, and any situation where an animal reads as natural and a human reads as threat.
  • Beast Speech and Speak with Animals give her access to testimony that no other investigator can obtain. Creatures remember events without the noise of human narrative.
  • Her spell suite - Sense Spirit, Sense Life, Sense Foes, Forest Warning, Sense Emotion, and Hide Path - makes her an extraordinarily capable advance scout in rural, estate, and contested landscape cases.
  • She is most distinctive in campaigns that tend toward urban and indoor scenes: she keeps the case from forgetting that the disturbance did not begin when the first respectable witness noticed it.

Why She Works

Evelyn proves that nature magic in 1923 can be grounded, disciplined, and narratively irreplaceable rather than decorative pastoral fantasy. She does not make the countryside prettier. She makes it load-bearing evidence.

Character Overview

Evelyn Alder learned the world through weather, animal movement, old paths, and the kind of warning most settled people dismiss until they need it. Long before anyone put a more formal name on what she was becoming, she already understood that land is not mute, that wilderness is not empty, and that older rites survive longest in places modern authority has mistaken for irrelevant. She was never meant for the comfortable illusion that civilization explains everything worth explaining. She grew into a woman who reads storms, beasts, tracks, groves, boundary places, and human trespass with the same seriousness other investigators bring to signatures, ledgers, or bloodstains.

That is what makes her such a strong FRH character. Evelyn does not merely fit the setting; she pulls on a version of it that is often latent in more urban campaigns. Around her, land, weather, animals, old rites, and rural metaphysics stop being atmosphere and become evidence. She belongs naturally in roads, wild corridors, damaged communities, haunted tracts, river towns, estate edges, and any case where the room is not the whole truth because the field, the tree line, the water, or the ridge remembers more. Her shapeshifting and ritual knowledge should never read as comic-book novelty. They read best as continuity with older practice and older responsibility.

What keeps Evelyn from collapsing into soft pastoral fantasy is that she is not a decorative nature mystic. She is observant, disciplined, and capable of bringing hard judgment to human institutions that believe their maps, fences, titles, or development plans have settled a place's meaning. She can widen a party's clue structure all by herself. In one group she turns an urban case toward fields, family plots, and older contamination. In another she keeps salons and museums from pretending they are detached from beasts, weather, and bargain-haunted ground. In still another she becomes the lead on place truth itself, the person who understands that the disturbance did not begin when the first respectable witness noticed it.

A new companion should understand Evelyn as a mediator between worlds that modern people prefer to separate. She is not anti-civilization, but neither is she fooled by it. She knows the countryside is not merely background to human drama, and that the land often remains the oldest, sternest, and most patient witness in the case.
