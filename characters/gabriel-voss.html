Gabriel Voss

A patient created being carrying old violence in a maintained body - steady where others panic, durable where others break, and more stubbornly human than his origin has any right to explain.

Points: 375 True Age: 104 (Apparent age 36) Public Read: Heavy-shouldered, wire-rimmed, calm man with suture traces and the stillness of someone who can stand in the line of fire without blinking Core Role: Physical anchor, moral continuity engine, and durable protector

Powers

  • Constructed body: Gabriel does not breathe, eat, drink, or sleep. He does not heal the way living beings do. When he is damaged he is repaired. He is immune to poison and disease and has lived for over a century without aging.
  • Extraordinary durability: Extra Hit Points +8, DR 6 (Everything), Ablative DR 12 (regenerates 1/day), Hard to Kill 4, High Pain Threshold, Unfazeable, and Strong Will +3 make him the party's single hardest thing to stop.
  • Weirdness Magnet: supernatural phenomena are drawn toward him. This is a liability as much as an asset, but it means he is often the first point of contact with the metaphysical layer of a case.
  • Longevity and deep memory: his true age is over a century. He has lived through events, met people, and accumulated observations that no other party member can match.

Required Secret Power

Constructed being / alchemical heavy. Gabriel's hidden lane is not a named psionic or magic power - it is his nature. The full cluster of inhuman advantages constitutes the secret that must be concealed:

  • Doesn't Breathe [20]; Doesn't Eat or Drink [10]; Doesn't Sleep [20]
  • Immunity to Poison [15]; Immunity to Disease [10]
  • Extra Hit Points +8 [40]; DR 6 (Everything) [18]; Ablative DR 12 (1/day) [12]
  • Hard to Kill 4 [20]; Longevity [5]; Unique [-5]

Secret (Constructed being / alchemical heavy) [-20]: his true nature is deeply dangerous to disclose. In 1923 America, discovery would draw either scientific exploitation or violent hostility - likely both.

Required Secret Society

No named non-FRH hidden order appears in the source sheet.

Gabriel's source does not list a Patron or institutional tie beyond FRH. His hidden social infrastructure - makers' journals, clandestine laboratories, old patrons, occult manufacture networks - is implied by his backstory but not formalized as a sheet advantage.

This is consistent with his design: he is a Unique character whose social existence is entirely constructed rather than inherited or organized. The absence may be intentional, or it may represent a sheet gap. The current page preserves the source as written and flags this for later human review.

FRH does not satisfy the secret-society requirement.

How Gabriel Uses His Powers

  • He is the party's physical constant - the man who absorbs fire, stays in the room, and does not go down when the situation gets irreversible. His durability creates operational space for everyone else.
  • His immunity to biological and environmental attack means he can enter poisoned, diseased, or otherwise lethal environments that would kill or incapacitate other investigators.
  • His Weirdness Magnet disadvantage makes him an early-warning system for the supernatural layer of a case - phenomena orient toward him before they become visible to others.
  • His century of lived experience makes him a historical reference point. He has context for events, families, places, and patterns that only a very old observer would carry.
  • His greatest investigative contribution is moral and psychological steadiness. He is difficult to panic, charm, or destabilize because he has been a target of making, classification, and misunderstanding for a hundred years. He does not impress easily and does not break easily.

Why He Works

Gabriel proves that a Frankenstein-derived created being can be the party's most stabilizing presence rather than its most disturbing one. His power is quiet protector energy and historical depth. He is not a monster. He is the experiment that outlived the right of anyone to call it an experiment.

Character Overview

Gabriel Voss was not born into the world in the ordinary sense, and that fact has defined every year of his long existence. He was made in Eastern Europe more than a century ago by hands that treated flesh, chemistry, will, and trespass as parts of the same experiment. Whatever name he had at the beginning, whatever body first supplied the material, whatever conscience the maker claimed in justification, all of that is buried now under the harder truth of continuity. Gabriel endures. He does not breathe, does not eat, does not sleep, and does not heal as other men do. When he is torn, he is not comforted by nature. He is repaired. His very body is an argument someone once made against the limits of life.

That could easily have made him grotesque in the shallow sense, but Gabriel is strongest in the opposite direction. He is grave, patient, durable, and deeply, stubbornly human in the places that matter. He has lived too long as an object of making, maintenance, speculation, or fear to treat personhood lightly. He knows what it means to be handled, classified, and misunderstood. He knows the difference between a wound and a failing seam, between exhaustion and structural damage, between being pitied and being recognized. In FRH he intensifies a core question the setting already wants to ask: what counts as a person in a world where soul, craft, belief, violence, and trespass can all leave fingerprints on the body?

Gabriel's life naturally draws the world around him into makers' journals, clandestine laboratories, private patrons, occult manufacture, and the ethics of repair. Yet he is not merely a walking problem statement. In party play he tends to become a moral and social continuity engine, the person who keeps a case from becoming only a puzzle or only a hunt. He understands damage in the practical sense and violation in the metaphysical sense, and he is difficult to impress with bad theories or fashionable panic. His power is quiet protector energy, not theatrical monstrosity. The more frightened a room becomes, the steadier he tends to look.

A new companion should understand Gabriel first as a man who has outlived the right of the experiment to continue and continues anyway. He is not an action figure stitched together out of horror props. He is a patient created being carrying old violence in a maintained body, trying to decide what decency looks like after manufacture, survival, and history have all had their turn at him.
