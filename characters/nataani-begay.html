Nataani Begay

Diné field antiquarian and deep-time psychometrist whose gift ties him to violated relics, disturbed ancestors, and the moral accounts institutions file under the language of discovery.

Points: 375 Age: 33 Public Read: Lean Diné surveyor-scholar with field-creased clothes and the self-control of someone used to being the least officially trusted expert in the room Core Role: Deep-time psychometrist, artifact investigator, provenance moralist, and custodial network anchor

Powers

  • Psychometry 22: the deepest temporal reach in the character library. At base he reads back 10,648 years (to approximately 8,700 BCE - pre-Neolithic, before Stonehenge, before the Great Pyramid, before Jericho's earliest cities). Physical range: 484" (~39 ft / ~13m) circle around the object.
  • Extra Effort reach: with maximum effort (-12 Fatigue, -8 Skill) he extends to 17,576 years (to approximately 15,650 BCE - late Paleolithic, Mesolithic, the end of the Last Ice Age, Paleo-Indian arrivals in the Americas).
  • Practical scope: any sacred object, burial good, artifact, ruin, or violated site can tell him what happened there, who handled it, and what the moral history of its possession looks like. He does not need documentation. The object is the testimony.
  • Anthropology-18, Archaeology-18, History skills, Law-18: his psychometric gift is backed by genuine scholarly and legal expertise that makes him lethal in institutional confrontations over custody and provenance.

Required Secret Power

Psychometry 22 [22].

This is not residue-reading at the ordinary human scale. Nataani's Psychometry reaches into geological time - well beyond any investigative use case that would be publicly comprehensible. A violated sacred site, a looted burial object, or a mishandled relic becomes a 10,000-year witness statement in his hands.

Operational skill: Psychometry-17 [6] (40'4" radius range, 10,648 year history).

Secret (Secret Psi) [-20]: the power is hidden and would be dangerous if recognized - both to the institutions he challenges and to the people who want the objects he can read.

Required Secret Society

Patron (Burial Keepers of the Taken Things) (9 or less) [10].

Nataani's non-FRH hidden-order lane is the Burial Keepers of the Taken Things - a custodial network dedicated to returning sacred objects and ancestors to their rightful keepers. FRH does not count toward this requirement.

Secret (Patron (Burial Keepers of the Taken Things)) [-5].

The network's existence and his membership are both concealed. Disclosing his affiliation would expose both him and the network to institutional suppression, theft prevention efforts, and supernatural retaliation from parties that benefit from the objects staying where they are.

How Nataani Uses His Powers

  • He reads provenance morally. A museum case label, an estate auction catalogue, or a university archive is a lie he can test by touching the object and reading the actual chain of custody going back thousands of years.
  • He is most effective in cases where the crime begins with misappropriation - stolen sacred objects, disturbed burials, falsified archaeological provenance, and supernatural events caused by objects removed from their ritual context.
  • His Anthropology-18, Archaeology-18, and Law-18 give him institutional standing to confront collectors, curators, and authorities in formal proceedings. He does not merely accuse - he builds a case.
  • His contact network (BIA investigator, Navajo Tribal Police, county records clerk) gives him ground-level intelligence across the Four Corners and Southwest that no urban-based investigator can match.
  • He is the party's witness for place truth that precedes any living human's memory - the person who understands not just what happened last year but what covenant was broken centuries or millennia ago that set the current case in motion.

Why He Works

Nataani proves that Psychometry at this power level is not just a clue tool - it is a moral instrument. He does not collect. He returns. The gift makes him more answerable to violated places and taken things, not more powerful over them.

Character Overview

Nataani Begay learned early that the language of preservation is often just theft spoken slowly and with credentials. He came up through hard-country work first: rail routes, survey discipline, horseflesh, field camps, and the practical education of a man who has spent real time outside institutions before having to negotiate with them. War, labor, and movement gave him the part of himself that cannot be bluffed by polite paperwork. The antiquarian in him grew on top of that older spine, not instead of it. By the time he began moving through museums, university collections, private archives, and field expeditions, he already knew too much about routes, custody, and who actually pays when a sacred thing is renamed a discovery.

That is what makes Nataani different from other artifact-facing characters. He is not driven by acquisition but by return. He is not a collector, and he is not impressed by collectors. He reads provenance morally. His psychometric gift ties him to violated relics, disturbed remains, false catalogues, and the small lies institutions tell themselves when they want possession to sound like stewardship. He can stand in front of a case label, a ruined site, or a badly handled object and feel that the wrongness is older and more human than the museum language surrounding it. That is why he reads routes, ruins, records, and bones with the same hard eye.

The hidden society around him, the Burial Keepers of the Taken Things, makes explicit what was already true in the man: he belongs to a custodial world rather than an extractive one. His professional life is therefore a constant argument with respectable theft. He knows rail schedules and survey maps, but he also knows family memory, burial duty, and what it means when institutions keep ancestors under the language of science, preservation, or civilization. He moves through their rooms because someone must. He studies them because someone should know exactly how the crime is being filed.

For a new player, Nataani should feel like a field-hardened Dine antiquarian whose scholarship has never become detached from labor, place, or obligation. His powers do not make him mystical in the cheap sense. They make him more answerable to violated sites and taken things. He is there to get sacred objects and the dead home if he can, and to make sure the people hiding behind glass and paper know that somebody noticed what they called legitimate.
