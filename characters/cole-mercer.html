Cole Mercer

Blues-route wanderer and room settler whose music is the vehicle for a psionic gift that heals bodies, reads rooms, and walks the astral plane.

Powers

  • Multi-lane music-bound psionic: Cole's gifts operate across three distinct lanes - astral access, direct healing, and emotional/mental reading - all expressed most naturally through music and performance.
  • Room and people reading: Emotion Sense-17, Detect Lies-22*, and Sense Aura-19 make him one of the best investigators of human truth and spiritual weather in the party.
  • Psionic healing: Psionic Healing-19 at Power 8 means he can repair real physical damage when music or focused attention creates the right channel.
  • Astral access: Astral Projection 3 with Astral Sight-16 lets him survey places and presences that other investigators cannot reach physically.

Key psionic skills: Astral Sight-16 [6]; Detect Lies-22* [10]; Emotion Sense-17 [8]; Psionic Healing-19 [12]; Sense Aura-19 [12].

Secret (Music-bound psionic gift) [-30]: the powers are concealed and would be dangerous if recognized. The musical framing disguises them but does not protect them if pressed.

Required Secret Society

Patron (Sanctified Chord Circle) (9 or less) [5]: Secret: -5.

The Sanctified Chord Circle is Cole's non-FRH hidden-order lane. FRH does not count toward this requirement.

The Circle appears to be a discreet network tied to music, labor routes, and psychically-gifted performers - consistent with Cole's operational geography and backstory, though the source sheet does not elaborate further on the Circle's full structure or origin.

How Cole Uses His Powers

  • He reads a room before the party knows it needs reading - emotional weather, bad presences, buried grief, and spiritual contamination all register through Emotion Sense, Sense Aura, and Detect Lies before anyone else has noticed.
  • He turns music into a precision instrument: when he plays at the right moment, memory, feeling, and buried truth have a way of surfacing whether the room wanted them to or not.
  • His astral access lets him survey locations, haunted spaces, and supernatural presences before physical entry - effective advance reconnaissance for cases with high metaphysical density.
  • His psionic healing is most effective in cases involving grief, psychic violation, spiritual damage, or trauma that has left a physical mark. He does not replace a physician, but he covers what a physician cannot.
  • His labor-route contact network, Area Knowledge, and Streetwise skills make him the party's best intelligence asset in working-class, migrant, and Black American social geography - places where most FRH investigators cannot go without drawing the wrong kind of attention.

Why He Works

Cole demonstrates that a psionic investigator expressed entirely through cultural identity and working-class geography can be more atmospherically coherent than a generic telepathic specialist. The music is not a gimmick. It is the mechanism.

Character Overview

Cole Mercer learned his trade in the places respectable histories skip over: rail sidings, labor camps, depot platforms, boarding houses, strike edges, bad roads, and juke joints where a man could earn a meal, a bed, a warning, or a beating depending on how the room was feeling. Music was never just decoration to him. It was work, witness, appetite, worship, bargaining, consolation, and sometimes the only thing keeping a crowd from breaking the wrong way. He became the sort of musician who listens before he plays, who can tell when a room wants laughter, when it wants grief, and when it wants the truth spoken sideways so people can bear to hear it at all.

Being a Black man moving that circuit in 1923 taught Cole exactly what kind of grace the world permits and what kind it punishes. He knows routes, rumors, labor politics, boarding house etiquette, who can be trusted near money, who can be trusted near sorrow, and which places are humming wrong before anyone else notices. He lives close to struggle and does not pretend otherwise. His hands are careful because his guitar is part livelihood, part companion, and part instrument in the older sense of the word: the thing through which something larger can sometimes pass. What first looked like instinct turned out to be a dangerous gift. Cole can read strain, lies, hurt, and bad spiritual weather through people and places, and when he sings or plays at the right moment, memory, feeling, and buried truth have a way of rising to the surface whether anyone in the room wanted them to or not.

What keeps Cole from becoming merely uncanny is that mercy is built into him as deeply as the music. He is not a flashy wonder-worker and not a spellcaster with a prop. He is a labor-route witness and room-settler, a man whose powers intensify who he already was: someone good in a crisis, good at reading people, good at carrying news, and unable to walk past the wounded, the desperate, or the nearly broken without trying to help. He will use the guitar when he has it, the harmonica when he does not, and whatever courage he has left when neither feels like enough. That is why people trust him where grief is close to the surface. He sounds like a musician, but he works like a witness.
