1870 Predecessor Party - Historical FRH Operative. Silas Brent belongs to the party that encountered the events the 1923 investigators inherit as residue, evidence, and unresolved consequence. He is not available as a 1923 player character.

Silas Brent

Route-and-ground truth man whose practical reading of movement, freight, telegraph, and frightened animals is the foundation - and whose Hellfire Shot 20 (3/day, gun-channeled fire 5d at range 50) is the unforgettable break that prevents anyone from reducing him to pure practicality.

Powers

  • Hellfire Shot 20(5) - fire 5d, Range 50, 3/day [55]: gun-channeled supernatural fire, Instantaneous (+20%), Full Power Only (-10%), Limited Use 3/day (-20%). He does not cast - he shoots, and what comes out of the barrel is not a bullet. At fire 5d against most 1870 targets, this is operationally decisive when it fires. The 3/day limit means it is a response to specific conditions, not a standing resource. He does not lead with it.
  • Danger Sense [15]: threat warning before visible indicators. In frontier terrain where ambush, weather, and animal sign all precede visible danger, this functions as a survival multiplier on top of his already exceptional tracking.
  • Alertness +4 [20]: among the highest passive perception scores in the historical party. Combined with Tracking-16 and Riding (Horse)-17, he reads ground, movement, and intent in ways other party members cannot approach.
  • Hard to Kill 2 [10]: +2 to HT survival rolls. He has survived the frontier by not going down easily, and the sheet reflects it mechanically as well as narratively.
  • Guns/TL5 (Pistol)-19 [4] and Guns/TL5 (Rifle)-18 [2]: the highest ranged weapon skills in the historical party. When the Hellfire Shot is not available, Silas is still the party's most accurate shooter by a significant margin.
  • Contact network - Telegraph Operator, Stablemaster/Freight Boss, County Sheriff/Deputy Marshal: his institutional reach runs through the infrastructure of western movement. He knows the people who move information, freight, and law across the frontier, and they know him.

Required Secret Power

Note: No Secret disadvantage for the Hellfire Shot appears in the source sheet.

Silas's only disadvantage is FRH Duty. Like Julian Ashcroft in the same party, he carries no Secret for his supernatural capability. The Hellfire Shot is either undisclosed without being formally concealed, openly demonstrated in frontier contexts where explanation was not sought, or a gap in the 1870 party's design template.

The power is presented here as written. If a Secret (Gun-channeled hellfire) [-20] should formally accompany it, that is a human review item.

  • Hellfire Shot 20(5) [55] - gun-channeled fire 5d, Instantaneous, Full Power Only, Limited Use 3/day

The mechanic (gun-channeled flame) implies the supernatural is indistinguishable from the mundane act of shooting until the results arrive. This may explain the absence of a formal secret: witnesses see a man shoot; what they cannot explain is the fire.

Required Secret Society

No named non-FRH Patron or secret-society affiliation appears in the source sheet.

Silas's single disadvantage is FRH Duty. He carries no named society beyond FRH, no fraternal order, no hidden organization. His network runs through frontier infrastructure - telegraph operators, stablehands, deputy marshals - which is relational and institutional but not a secret society in the formal sense.

Like Julian Ashcroft, this may be the correct design: a frontier operative whose loyalties run to ground, trail, and practical obligation rather than to organized orders. The current page preserves the source as written.

FRH does not satisfy the secret-society requirement.

How Silas Uses His Powers

  • Hellfire Shot fires at Guns/TL5 (Pistol)-19 - his base gun skill, not a separate shooting roll. He aims, he fires, and the result is fire 5d at range 50. In a frontier scenario, range 50 means he can fire from concealment. The limit is the 3/day cap - once spent, he is a very good shooter with conventional ammunition.
  • Danger Sense combined with Alertness +4 means he is almost never surprised in terrain he can read. Ambush, pursuit, and night approach are all significantly harder against Silas than against the rest of the party.
  • Tracking-16 at IQ+3 (8 points) is the skill he built over a career. He reads passage, disturbance, loaded freight, and the signs of frightened animals as evidence - the ground is a record to him that other party members cannot access.
  • Area Knowledge (Stage Routes and Depot Towns)-17 and Area Knowledge (Western Trails and Rail Cuts)-17 give him the geographic intelligence to reconstruct where people went and how, not just what they left behind.
  • His contact network - telegraph operator, stablemaster, sheriff - gives him the institutional reach to confirm route intelligence, cross-reference timelines, and call on law enforcement assistance in frontier jurisdictions.
  • Detect Lies-15 at IQ+2 means he reads witness reliability clearly. He knows when a story and the facts have stopped belonging to each other before the party has fully assembled the evidence.

Why He Works

Silas proves that a frontier tracker reads cleanest in FRH when the supernatural element rises from the practical foundation rather than replacing it. His hellfire is not his identity - his identity is reading what the ground, the line, and the frightened living already know. The fire is the one impossible sign that proves the world got stranger than the old hands ever promised. He begins from tracks, freight, ash, and fear. The hell comes after.

Character Overview

Silas Brent is the kind of man frontier country manufactures when it has more danger than explanation and no patience for either complaint or ornament. He learned truth through ground, weather, freight, telegraph lines, frightened animals, bad camps, and the look on a witness's face when the story has already lost contact with what happened. Long before the supernatural made itself impossible to ignore, Silas had already built a life around the practical reading of movement: who passed, what they carried, where they lied, what the trail refused to hide, and how quickly trouble could move from rumor into blood.

That makes him invaluable to the historical FRH party. Silas is a route-and-ground truth man first. He notices depots, stage routes, freight pressure, camp logic, telegraph intelligence, violence residue, and danger before explanation. Then comes the unforgettable break in him: the hellfire that prevents anyone from reducing him to pure practicality. That supernatural fact matters, but it works best because it rises out of a man whose fundamental gift is still reading what the ground, the line, and the frightened living already know.

Silas should not be romanticized into a clean cowboy emblem. He is weathered, suspicious, direct, and more comfortable with ugly necessity than polished theory. The frontier taught him not only endurance but ranking: which witness can be trusted, which animal sign is fresh, which trail is false, which campfire was nervous, which story was written by somebody who has never had to survive the road it describes. In an archive shaped by stranger minds, he keeps movement, risk, and practical consequence in the frame.

To a new companion, Silas should read as the man who knows where the bodies, the wagons, and the lies all went even before anyone has decided what to call the thing that scared them. He does not begin from symbolism. He begins from tracks, freight, ash, fear, and the one impossible sign that proves the world has gotten stranger than the old hands ever promised.
