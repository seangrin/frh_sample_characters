Edwin March

Broad-shouldered, worn, and too still when at rest - a workingman shaped by mud, wire, bombardment, and bad orders. Industrial labor before the war; the war itself; and whatever the war opened in him that came home with the man but does not entirely belong to him.

Points: 375 Age: 31 Height/Weight: 5'10", 175 lbs Public Read: British infantry survivor with a worker's hands and a survivor's stillness. Notices tools, vehicles, weak points, and room quality before he notices grand theory. Powers

  • Alternate Form (Trench Survival Organism) [264]: the entire weight of Edwin's secret in a single advantage. His body carries a second form - ST 18, DR 10, Invulnerability (Bullets), Claws/Talons, Berserk, Bloodlust - that emerges under sufficient pressure. The [264] point cost represents the full mechanical weight of the Trench Form relative to his human baseline. The form is player-controlled. Edwin's player enters and directs it; see the separate Trench Form sheet for complete stats.
  • Fit [5]: HT +1 to recovery; double Fatigue recovery rate. The war conditioned him toward endurance; this is the mechanical residue of that conditioning.
  • Strong Will +2 [8] - Will 15: the self-command required to hold the Trench Form in check is not separate from this advantage. He does not go down easily in a contest of will.
  • Combat Reflexes [15]: Fright Check 17. He has been in enough violence that the threat-response is trained, not improvised. He does not freeze; he assesses.
  • Patron (Royal British Legion - Secret) (9 or less) [5]: the Legion connection is real but infrequent - rolls of 9 or less, not 15. It gives him access to a veteran network with institutional reach: inquiries across the British diaspora, quiet support, and the credibility of a recognized name in right company. See Required Secret Society.

Required Secret Power

Alternate Form (Trench Survival Organism) [264] is the secret. The entire Trench Form is what Edwin is hiding.

  • Secret (Trench Form) [-20] - explicit concealment of the alternate form
  • Addiction (Trench Form) [-25] - Incapacitating or Hallucinogenic; Totally Addictive; Legal +5. The form exerts pull between transformations - it is not simply a crisis response but a compulsion that costs him something to resist

Discovery of the Trench Form ends every social and institutional position Edwin holds simultaneously - Legion connection, FRH operative status, and any civilian standing in one moment.

Required Secret Society

Patron (Royal British Legion - Secret) (9 or less) [5]: the Legion affiliation is present both as a Patron advantage and as a Secret disadvantage.

Secret (Patron [Royal British Legion - Secret]) [-5] accompanies it. The net cost of the affiliation is effectively zero - the [5] Patron is offset by the [-5] Secret - but the organizational connection is real and carries weight when it fires.

The Royal British Legion is a named, organized veterans' institution with genuine reach across the British community in 1923. It is a real secret society in the formal sense - an organized body with its own agenda, resources, and knowledge of Edwin's situation. FRH does not satisfy this requirement.

How Edwin Uses His Capabilities

  • Soldier/TL6-15 is the highest skill on his sheet - it is how he was comprehensively trained and the lens through which he reads situations. He sees fields, positions, angles, and supply lines before he sees people.
  • Holdout-14 and Stealth-14 are his concealment capabilities. In a 1923 context these are practical PI and operative skills - he can move without announcing himself and carry what he needs without advertising it.
  • Streetwise-14 and Leadership-14 give him access to working-class institutional networks and the authority to direct people in a crisis. These are not glamorous skills; they are durable ones.
  • Mechanic/TL6 (Automobile)-14 and Driving/TL6 (Automobile)-14 together mean he understands vehicles mechanically and operationally. In 1923, this is a significant practical advantage - most people can ride; fewer can diagnose and fix what breaks.
  • The Trench Form is available to Edwin's player as an option under sufficient pressure. The Addiction (Trench Form) means he may also be pulled toward it between investigations. The form is not a resource to be deployed casually - its aftermath is usually an investigative problem in itself.
  • Research-13 at IQ+0 (2 points) is the residue of purpose: an investigative drive that runs through both forms. Even in the Trench Form at IQ 11, Research remains at 13. This is the one skill that refuses to fully degrade.

Investigative Role

  • Practical field intelligence - terrain, supply, vehicle, position
  • Veteran network access through the Royal British Legion

  • Witness reading - Streetwise and Detect Lies are absent from his sheet, but Soldier/TL6 and the Sense of Duty together mean he reads desperation, fear, and bad orders clearly

Why He Works

Edwin works in FRH because the Trench Form is not his identity - it is his wound made physical. The Addiction (Trench Form) is the detail that prevents the transformation from being merely a superpower: it has weight between activations, not just during them. The man who comes back from the form has to account for what the form did. That accounting is where Edwin's investigative competence, his veteran network, and his Obsession all converge into something more than a monster-with-a-day-job.

Character Overview

Before the war, Edwin March was the sort of man history is usually happy to use up without remembering. He came out of industrial labor, knew machines by their noise and their temper, and understood the dignity of work in terms of weight, heat, grease, and wages honestly earned. Then the Great War took that practical life and fed it into the infantry. Edwin crossed from factory discipline into trench discipline the way thousands of men did: not as a hero from the beginning, but as a workingman who learned mud, wire, bombardment, fatigue, and the long education of bad orders.

What came home from the war was not exactly the same man who had gone out. Somewhere between trench rot, shellfire, ruin, and whatever impossible thing the war opened in him, his body learned a second answer to danger. Under enough pressure it becomes something inhuman: a trench-born organism of survival, hunger, and mutilated instinct. Edwin does not think of that form as another life. It is the same wound wearing another shape, the same man dragged into a state he fears, hides, and sometimes needs. What makes him tragic is not that he turns monstrous, but that the monstrous thing is still built out of the laborer and soldier he once was.

That is why Edwin remains so grounded even at his strangest. He still thinks like a practical man. He notices tools, vehicles, weak points, supply problems, and the quality of a room before he notices anyone's grand theory. He knows what bodies can endure and what they cannot. He has the fellowship of damaged veterans, the wariness of a man who does not entirely trust his own skin, and the habits of someone who has spent years trying to stay useful while concealing a truth that would turn him into a specimen, a scandal, or a hunted thing.

To a new companion, Edwin should read first as a British infantry survivor with a worker's hands and a survivor's stillness, and only then as a man with a terrible second form. He is not a monster with a human disguise. He is a human being who should have come home damaged and instead came home altered, carrying the sort of war that does not stay in memory but moves into flesh.
