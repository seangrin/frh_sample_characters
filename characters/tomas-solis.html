Tomas Solis

Treasury agent and solar protector - the man in the room most likely to take the hit, hold the line, and keep the worst thing from reaching everyone else, even when it costs him sunlight to recharge the means.

Points: 375 Age: 42 Public Read: White-passing federal agent with severe tailoring, wire-rimmed glasses, and the unreadable stillness of a man who knows more than he says Core Role: Federal investigator, solar force-field protector, and institutional x-ray operator

Powers

  • DR 30 Solar Force Field: in emergencies, a gold shimmer appears where impact should have landed. The field extends to protect others (Affects Others: +40%), can be deployed as a wall (Wall: +30%), and functions instantaneously (+20%). It is visible (gold shimmer, leaves a mental signature), emergency-only, and powered exclusively by sunlight.
  • Sunlight recharge: Extra Fatigue +10 powers the force field and recharges only in sunlight (1 hour charge cycle). This creates an operational dependency on daily sun exposure that functions as a genuine tactical constraint.
  • Legal Enforcement Powers [10]: his federal authority is real and operational, not merely cover. He can compel cooperation, access records, and act in official capacity in ways no private investigator can.
  • Danger Sense [15]: field instinct that functions as a threat warning ahead of visible indicators.

FRH Package

  • Patron (Followers of the Right Hand) (15 or less) [60]: Equipment: Standard, +5; Special Qualities (Access to Magic in Non-magical World): Very Unusual, +10; Secret: -5; Subtraction (Minimal Intervention): -5.
  • Duty (15 or less) [-25]: Involuntary: -5; Subtraction (Extremely Hazardous): -5.
  • Enemy (Unknown Enemies of FRH) [-60]: Unknown: -5; Subtraction (Access to Magic in Non-magical World): -15.
  • Duty (Treasury Agent, 12 or less) [-5]: a second, non-dangerous duty to his official employer that runs parallel to his FRH obligation.

Required Secret Power

Solar shield powers:

  • DR 30 (Everything) [171] - the core field, with extensive cost modifiers: Affects Others +40%; Area Effect +50% (1 hex); Wall +30%; Instantaneous +20%; Emergencies Only -30%; Leaves Mental Signature -10%; Nuisance Effect (gold shimmer) -10%
  • Extra Fatigue +10 [15] - exclusively for powering the force field; only recharges in sunlight (1 hour); leaves a mental signature; cannot be used for any other purpose

Secret (Solar shield powers) [-20]: the ability is hidden. A visible supernatural power deployed by a federal agent in 1923 America is not merely a personal risk - it is an institutional and political crisis if disclosed.

Required Secret Society

Secret (Treasury back-channel mandate) [-5].

Tomas operates with an undisclosed secondary mandate within his Treasury work - a back-channel that operates outside normal reporting structures. The source sheet does not formalize this as a named Patron advantage; it appears only as a Secret disadvantage.

This may represent a covert government channel rather than a traditional secret society. If a named non-FRH Patron should be attached to this lane, it would need human review.

FRH does not satisfy the secret-society requirement.

How Tomas Uses His Powers

  • The solar force field is most valuable in civilian-protection scenarios - when the party needs someone to take the hit, hold the line, or keep dangerous impact from reaching a weaker investigator or innocent. At DR 30, he can stand between most 1923-era threats and absorb them.
  • The field's protection of others (Affects Others: +40%) and wall deployment mode mean he can shield more than himself in the right circumstances - but the Emergencies Only restriction means it does not run continuously.
  • His sunlight recharge dependency requires planning. A party running extended indoor or nighttime operations needs to account for when Tomas last had an hour of direct sunlight.
  • His Legal Enforcement Powers let him access courthouses, federal records, rail depots, and official channels that private investigators cannot enter without extraordinary social manipulation.
  • His contact network (bootleg fixer, city desk reporter, courthouse clerk, police matron) gives him cross-sector intelligence that bridges official and unofficial city geographies.

Why He Works

Tomas proves that solar inheritance expressed as a protective force rather than an offensive weapon reads cleanly in FRH. He did not ask for the gift. Sunlight made a claim on him anyway. He remains a government man, but no longer only that.

Character Overview

Tomas Solis spent most of his adult life becoming the kind of federal man other officials trust when matters are delicate, politically dangerous, or embarrassing enough that no one wants to write the real reason down. He built that reputation through patience, discretion, and the difficult habit of seeing clearly without hurrying to display it. In official rooms he reads as what he in many ways is: seasoned, formal, self-controlled, and quietly formidable. He understands institutions, courthouses, depots, clerks, witness statements, frightened local officials, and the ways a government can begin lying to itself long before it lies to the public. Men above him value him because he notices when the story and the facts stop belonging to each other.

What they do not fully understand is that there is another inheritance moving inside him, older and less legible than the Treasury. Tomas carries deep Mexican indigenous roots and a solar line of force that does not make him a magician so much as a protector. His gift is not spectacle. It is intervention. Steel, glass, impact, heat, and bullets meet resistance where there should be none when he chooses to stand between danger and another person. He does not think of himself as a saint or a wizard, and the power itself seems almost to agree. It shows up most clearly in restraint, sacrifice, and the refusal to let harm pass through him into someone more vulnerable.

That combination makes him one of the cleanest FRH characters in the batch. He naturally bridges bureaucracy and the supernatural, public-order pressure and private occult knowledge, recurring civic cases and the deeper question of who inside the system is still acting in good faith. He works especially well in stories of courthouses, morgues, rail movement, backchannel memoranda, official narratives that do not fit the evidence, and dangerous things contained just before they become public disasters.

To a new companion, Tomas should feel like a man who expected to spend his life serving institutions and discovered, late and unwillingly, that sunlight itself had made a claim on him. He remains a government man, but no longer only that. Somewhere between federal duty and solar inheritance he became the person in the room most likely to take the hit, hold the line, and keep the worst thing from reaching everyone else.
