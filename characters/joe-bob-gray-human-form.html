Joe Bob Gray

Human Form

New Orleans private investigator - tall, plain-featured, well-built. Works cases of infidelity, fraud, and disappearance during the day; manages an uncontrollable lycanthropic curse during the hours between. The PI shell is the best available cover for a man who needs to follow trouble, read desperation, and keep his own mouth shut.

Points: 375 Age: 35 Height/Weight: 6'1", 180 lbs Public Read: Medium mustache, plain brown hair and eyes, well-built. Presents as competent, Southern, and guarded. The ugliness in his politics is not immediately visible but it is present. Core Role: New Orleans PI, Secret Sons of the South operative, uncontrolled werewolf - trying to solve himself before the state, the town, or the beast gets there first

Powers

  • Alternate Form (Werewolf) [296]: the largest single item on his sheet and the primary fact of his life. ST 28, IQ 7, Regeneration 1 HT/turn (not silver/magic), Mute, Amnesia (Doesn't Remember Being Human), Berserk, Bloodlust, Super Running 17 mph. The werewolf form is GM-controlled - when Joe Bob changes, the character does not remember being human, does not know the party as party, and cannot be directed by the player. See the separate Werewolf Form sheet.
  • Uncontrolled Change 4 [-50]: the change is not voluntary. With Uncontrolled Change 4 and the associated Reaction -4, this is the most significant and dangerous aspect of the curse - not the form itself but the loss of any reliable control over when it appears. The cure he believes exists is the goal that keeps him operational; without that belief the case for functioning as a PI collapses.
  • Combat Reflexes [15]: Fright Check 15. A PI in New Orleans has been in enough bad rooms that the threat response is trained. He does not freeze in his human form even when the curse pressure is active.
  • Lang: French (Accented) [4]: the operational language of New Orleans; he works cases in both languages and the French access gives him reach into communities that would be closed to a monolingual operator.

Required Secret Power

Alternate Form (Werewolf) [296] is the secret. The curse is the entire hidden identity.

  • Secret (Uncontrollable Werewolf) [-20] - the explicit concealment advantage. In 1923 New Orleans, exposure means imprisonment, mob violence, exile, or death. The secrecy is not theatrical; it is survival.

The Uncontrolled Change 4 modifier means the concealment is also structurally precarious - the change is not voluntary, so the secret cannot be kept through discipline alone. He has routines, habits, and self-containment structures built around managing the timing; they are imperfect by design.

Required Secret Society

Patron (Secret Sons of the South) (12 or less) [30]: Equipment: Standard, +5; Secret: -5.

The Secret Sons of the South is a named hidden organization - a Confederate-legacy fraternal order with its own agenda, resources, and knowledge of Joe Bob's situation. It is a real secret society in the formal sense: an organized body that provides material support (Equipment: Standard) in exchange for ongoing obligation. Rolls on 12 or less makes it more reliably accessible than a minor Patron.

A second secret accompanies the affiliation: Secret (Is a White Supremist / Confederacy Supporter) [-10]. The organization's political identity is itself a secret from the FRH hierarchy and the broader party - he hides both the organization and what it stands for. FRH does not satisfy the secret-society requirement.

How Joe Bob Operates

  • Detect Lies-14 at IQ+1 (6 points) is the most expensive single skill on his sheet. A PI whose livelihood depends on reading clients, witnesses, and informants has built this capability deliberately. In a 1923 New Orleans context, knowing when the story has lost contact with the facts is an operational necessity.
  • Shadowing-14 and Stealth-14 give him the fieldcraft to follow subjects without being burned. These are core PI capabilities - he is not primarily a combat operative but a surveillance and information-gathering one.
  • Area Knowledge (New Orleans)-15 at 4 points is the foundation of his local authority. He knows the neighborhoods, the routes, the informal power structures, and the places where things get lost or hidden. In cases set in New Orleans, this advantage is what makes him irreplaceable.
  • Fast-Talk-14 backs the PI cover - he can manage a witness, deflect an interviewer, or maintain a cover story at the same skill level he detects deception in others.
  • Guns/TL6 across Pistol/Rifle/Shotgun all at skill 15 makes him a competent shooter by 1923 standards. The Springfield M1903 is on his equipment list; this is not a man who relies only on a sidearm.
  • The Uncontrolled Change 4 is the governing constraint on everything else. The Move 3 anomaly in the source suggests an additional movement-impairing effect not explicitly listed - this is preserved as written. All plans involving Joe Bob must account for the possibility that the form appears before the case closes.

Investigative Role

  • New Orleans local authority - Area Knowledge, French, street network
  • Surveillance and field intelligence - Shadowing, Stealth, Detect Lies
  • Client and witness management - Fast-Talk, Savoir-Faire, Merchant

Why He Works

Joe Bob works in FRH because the lycanthropy and the ugliness are structurally connected rather than coincidentally adjacent. The secret society, the uncontrollable curse, and the moral rot all reinforce each other: a man with this much to hide is going to become expert at hiding things, and the same skills that make him a functional PI make him a plausible operator in a world where everyone is concealing something. The tragedy is not that he's a werewolf. The tragedy is that his best self and his worst self are equally real, and the curse does not distinguish between them.

Character Overview

Joe Bob Gray works as a private investigator in New Orleans, which means he spends his days prying into other people's lies while guarding one enormous lie of his own. From an early age he learned that anger, fear, or the wrong sort of pressure could strip him out of his human mind and throw him into an animal state that remembers very little except hunger, violence, and the crooked instinct to keep moving toward unfinished business. He did not grow into the curse gracefully. He grew around it the way men grow around a shotgun scar, by building routines, bad habits, and self-containment wherever there is room.

Joe Bob is not a pleasant man, and the project does him no favors by pretending otherwise. He carries ugly politics, inherited hatred, and the sort of Southern Gothic damage that makes moral rot and bodily transformation feel uncomfortably at home beside each other. That ugliness should not be softened into charm, but neither should it be allowed to replace the rest of him. He is still competent, still cagey, still frightened by what he becomes, and still committed enough to the idea of a cure that he keeps trying to function in the world instead of simply giving himself over to the curse.

The secrecy around him is not theatrical. In 1923, exposure would mean imprisonment, mob violence, exile, or death, and he knows it. The werewolf form is therefore not a second polished identity - only the catastrophic expression of the same life. When Joe Bob changes, the GM takes over, because the man who returns is no longer fully in command of what emerges. Joe Bob's real biography belongs to the human investigator who keeps trying to stay ahead of the monster, not to the monster itself.

For a new player, Joe Bob should feel like a classic uncontrollable werewolf translated into FRH's social and historical reality: a damaged investigator, morally compromised and often ugly, who is still trying to solve himself before the state, the town, or the beast gets there first.
