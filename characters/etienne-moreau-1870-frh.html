Etienne Moreau

Weathered Gulf privateer, survivor, and smuggler with Magery 2 water-and-weather magic - the man who can smell a false manifest, read harbor silence as bad luck, and call a Windstorm when polite options have run out.

Powers

  • Magery 2 [25]: full magical capacity expressed through water, weather, and air - a maritime sorcerer whose spell suite is shaped entirely by a life at sea. Where other mages work with fire, illusion, or mind, Etienne works with fog, current, wind, and the sea's own resistance to human plans.
  • Spell suite - Windstorm-20 and Fog-20 at highest: Create Air-18, Create Water-19, Fog-20, Purify Air-17, Purify Water-18, Seek Water-18, Shape Air-18, Windstorm-20. The combat application is real but narrow - fog for concealment, windstorm for force. The investigative applications are broader: water-finding, air-reading, and the navigation advantages that come from bending local weather to confirmed need.
  • Chronolocation [15]: he can identify where in time he is with unusual precision. In an investigation involving false dates, forged manifests, or displaced cargo timelines, this has direct operational value.
  • Absolute Direction [5]: never lost at sea or in harbor. Relevant in any scenario involving route reconstruction, false navigation, or mapping disputes.
  • Seamanship-18, Navigation-16, Merchant-17, Shiphandling-15: his non-magical maritime competence is itself investigative. He reads cargo logic, harbor customs, shipping routes, and the economic geography of the Gulf world at a level no other party member can match.

Required Secret Power

Note: No explicit Secret disadvantage for Etienne's magic appears in the source sheet.

His disadvantages are Bloodlust, Bully, Duty (FRH), and Greed - none of which cover the Magery 2 and spell suite as a concealed gift. This may reflect the 1870 party's earlier design template, a deliberately public magical reputation in Gulf/Caribbean contexts, or a sheet gap.

The magic is presented here as it appears in the source: operational without a named secret. If a Secret (Maritime sorcerer) [-20] should formally accompany it, that is a human review item.

  • Magery 2 [25] - full magical capacity from maritime inheritance
  • Chronolocation [15] - temporal self-location ability
  • Spell suite at skill 18-20 - water, weather, and air magic at high operational level

Required Secret Society

No named non-FRH Patron or secret-society affiliation appears in the source sheet.

Etienne's social infrastructure - smuggling networks, old Gulf contacts, ports-and-captains relationships - exists through skills, Contacts, Reputation, and character description rather than a formalized Patron advantage. His Contacts are individual (Customs Clerk, Harbormaster's Clerk, Wharf Broker) rather than organizational.

This may be the correct design for a man whose loyalties run to routes and relationships rather than institutions. Or it may be a 1870-party design gap. The current page preserves the source as written.

FRH does not satisfy the secret-society requirement.

How Etienne Uses His Powers

  • Fog-20 is concealment-on-demand at a harbor, coastal, or open-water scale. In 1870 with limited lighting and no radar, a well-placed fog is operationally decisive.
  • Windstorm-20 is the force option: not a combat spell in normal use, but capable of disrupting ship movement, open-water pursuit, or outdoor encounters at a level that reshapes the physical environment of the scene.
  • Create Water-19 and Purify Water-18 have direct survival applications in coastal and maritime scenarios where fresh water is controlled, poisoned, or disputed.
  • Chronolocation in combination with Seamanship-18 and Navigation-16 makes him the party's most reliable anchor for reconstructing when something happened on the water - arrival times, departure windows, weather conditions on specific dates.
  • Merchant-17 and Detect Lies-16 make him the party's best evaluator of commercial fraud, false manifests, and cargo-level deception. The Gulf archive of smuggling and imperial trade is in his head and his spell list simultaneously.
  • Bloodlust and Greed are real operational constraints. He is not neutral or safe. His disadvantages shape every negotiation and every scene where money is visible.

Why He Works

Etienne proves that a maritime sorcerer reads cleanest in FRH when the magic is an extension of route knowledge rather than a replacement for it. He notices false manifests, obsolete place names, customs lies, and weather truth at the same level of skill whether he is doing it with Seamanship-18 or Seek Water-18. In a group full of stranger gifts, he grounds the archive in movement, trade, and the ways power actually traveled.

Character Overview

Etienne Moreau came out of the Gulf world by way of routes honest men usually discuss only after they are old enough not to fear the consequences. He has been sailor, privateer, smuggler, survivor, and too many other things that look noble or criminal depending on how far away the listener is standing. By 1870 he is no longer young enough to pretend that the sea ennobles men on its own. What it has given him instead is compression: of memory, of weather, of route knowledge, of lies told in six ports under three flags, and of the exact look a cargo manifest gets when it was written to disguise something that should never have crossed a dock in the first place.

That makes him invaluable to the historical FRH party. Etienne reads old-water truth, harbor cunning, imperial residue, survivor's exaggeration, and route impossibility better than most men read a ledger. He notices false manifests, obsolete place names, customs lies, smuggling logic, maritime weather truth, and the way empire leaves stains in language long after the official map has changed. He is one of those men whose stories sound embellished until a second witness, a damaged document, or an old port habit proves that he was understating the wrong part.

He is not a moral innocent, and he should not be cleaned up into one. Greed, concealment, old loyalties, old grudges, and the practiced habit of authority all belong to him. Yet those same flaws give him shape. Etienne is strongest when the case touches cargo, coast, ownership, ports, routes, or imperial interference, because then his whole life becomes evidence instead of ornament.

To a new companion, Etienne should feel like a weathered Frenchman who has lived too long with water, danger, and compromised trade to confuse respectability with truth. He is the kind of man who can smell fraud in a shipping story, bad luck in a harbor silence, and history in the wrong kind of map, and he carries all three as if they were simply part of becoming old without becoming harmless.
