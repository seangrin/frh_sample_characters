The Weight of What You've Seen

FRH operatives encounter things that human minds were not designed to process. These rules govern the cost. They operate in layers — each layer more lasting than the last:

  1. Fright Checks — the immediate mechanic triggered by specific encounters. Roll and apply results from the Fright Check Table.
  2. Stress and Derangement — cumulative mental strain. Failed Fright Checks accumulate. Left unchecked, they convert into permanent disadvantage points.
  3. Insanity — an overlay that adds specific breakdown conditions to Fright Check results. The madness should echo the trauma that caused it.
  4. Corruption — the price of wielding certain supernatural powers. Separate from fear, but related in its downward spiral.

The GM applies these rules as the scene and character demand. Not every frightening encounter triggers every layer. The source for all of these mechanics is GURPS Horror (4th Edition), pp. 139–148, adapted for use in FRH's 3rd Edition framework.

Fright Checks

A Fright Check is a roll against Will, modified by circumstances and character traits. It is the core game mechanic for horror in GURPS. The GM sets the modifier; the character rolls; the Fright Check Table determines the result.

What Triggers a Fright Check

Common triggers in FRH:

  • Seeing a monster, entity, or creature that should not exist
  • Discovering fresh evidence of supernatural violence (freshly killed victims, impossible wounds)
  • Witnessing a death under disturbing or unnatural circumstances
  • Learning something that cannot be reconciled with a normal understanding of the world (see Forgotten Lore)
  • Encountering physical events that violate natural law

Perceived threat is what matters, not actual threat. A creature miles away that can reach you in seconds gives no bonus for distance.

Ad-Hoc Fright Checks

Stopping play to roll, look up, and apply Fright Checks can sometimes break the mood. As an alternative, the GM may assign 1d seconds of stun and continue. After the scene, players roll true Fright Checks and receive any quirks, disadvantages, or other lasting effects. Both parties must agree to use this approach.

Forgotten Lore and Awful Truths

In FRH, horror does not come only from monsters. Discovering information that cannot be reconciled with a normal understanding of the world can require a Fright Check. The more completely a discovery reveals an Awful Truth — and the more directly it threatens the character's world, identity, or safety — the greater the penalty.

Multiple and Cumulative Fright Checks

Sequential Fright Checks

When a gradually revealed horror forces several Fright Checks, apply each new modifier to only one Fright Check — they are not cumulative with each other. The modifier for the second check does not carry over to the third. Repeated Fright Checks can still take a toll, however.

Cumulative Effects of Fear

As the session wears on and failed checks accumulate, resistance erodes:

  • Each failed Fright Check imposes −1 to all subsequent Fright Checks in that adventure.
  • Each successful Fright Check removes −1 from this running penalty as the character "regains composure" — but never above zero.
  • The cumulative penalty resets between adventures, assuming the investigators get reasonable rest between them.

Some Fright Check Table results — notably new Phobias and permanent IQ loss — additionally and permanently affect future Fright Checks. These are in addition to the cumulative modifier.

For a more structured model of accumulating fear and lasting harm, see Stress and Derangement below.

Stress and Derangement

This system separates ordinary fear from sanity-threatening horror by dividing Fright Checks into two categories:

  • Ordinary Fright Checks — cause Stress (temporary).
  • Sanity-Blasting Fright Checks — cause Derangement (lasting).

The GM designates which type applies based on the nature of the trigger. Seeing a man with a knife is frightening but ordinary. Seeing what the cult has been doing in the basement of the hotel for twenty years may not be.

Tracking the Penalty

On a failed Fright Check: add −1 to Stress (ordinary) or Derangement (sanity-blasting). On a critical failure, add −3 instead.

The character has a cumulative penalty to all Fright Checks equal to (Stress + Derangement) ÷ 2, rounded against the character. Track Stress and Derangement separately — they recover at different rates.

Stress

Stress represents temporary jitters from being startled. It is shed at −1 per 10 minutes of mental relaxation — any situation that would give +4 or better for "routine tasks": target shooting, a Sunday drive, a quiet brandy in a safe room. Certain indulgences (massage, sex) may erase an extra point at GM discretion.

The GM may apply Stress penalties beyond Fright Checks:

  • Self-control rolls — Stress can make Bad Temper flare or turn a drink into a binge
  • Skill rolls requiring a steady hand (Explosives, Surgery)
  • HT rolls for physical performance under stress

Fearlessness can offset Stress penalties, but it cannot provide a net bonus above zero on a genuine Fright Check.

Stress Limit: A character's Stress limit is −Will (e.g., −12 for Will 12). Once Stress exceeds this limit, additional points convert directly into Derangement.

Derangement

Derangement represents lasting mental harm from sanity-blasting Fright Checks, overflowing Stress, bad drugs, sentient brain parasites, malevolent magic, or evil experiments — depending on what the campaign has brought the character into contact with.

At the end of any day that brought no new Stress or Derangement, the character may roll against Will. Success sheds −1 Derangement. A therapist with Psychology (Clinical) or Physician (Psychiatric) at 12+ gives +1 to this roll and may also roll against their own skill once per day to erase another −1 independently.

The GM may apply Derangement as a penalty to Influence rolls — a loose grip on reality is harder to conceal when actively trying to manipulate someone than when just sitting in a room. Derangement also penalizes any roll required to establish sanity (before a judge, before an asylum review board). Fearlessness never offsets Derangement.

Derangement Limit: A character's Derangement limit is also −Will. When Derangement exceeds this limit, additional points convert into permanent mental disadvantage points. This lowers the character's point value and requires treatment and earned character points to remove.

The Downward Spiral

Each failed Fright Check makes the next one harder to pass. Accumulated Stress makes self-control harder across the board. When Stress overflows into Derangement, the recovery window narrows. When Derangement overflows into permanent disadvantages, the character's point value falls and the new traits must be bought off through play. You can get rid of the shakes with a hot bath. Lasting craziness takes work. And permanent craziness costs points.

Treatment shortcut: Any cure capable of removing −1 point of full mental disadvantage can alternatively remove −2 Derangement or all accumulated Stress.

Insanity: The Madness Overlay

Insanity isolates. It leaves the sufferer uncertain: Did I actually experience that, or am I losing it? The madness overlay connects Fright Check results to specific, period-appropriate breakdown conditions. The goal is not a realistic simulation of mental illness, but a dramatically appropriate and thematically resonant result — one that echoes the trauma that caused it.

Applying the Overlay

Whenever a Fright Check is called for, the Fright Check Table result stands. Then apply the following overlay:

Fright Check Table ResultMadness DurationSelf-Control NumberLowers Point Value?
Result measured in seconds (results 4–12, 14–16) 2d seconds 6 No
Result measured in minutes or hours (results 17–21, 26–29, 31, 33) 1d hours or 1d days (GM's discretion) 9 No
Drastic or permanent result (results 13, 22–25, 30, 32, 34–40+) Permanent until bought off or treated Varies Yes

The GM may replace the Fright Check Table result with the madness condition, combine both, or adjust for story purposes. For permanent results, the replacement or overlay should have the same total disadvantage value as the original result. The GM may also make a condition recurring: whenever the character fails a Fright Check, they suffer from the same syndrome again — a thematic scar tied to a specific horror.

Cause

The source of a mental condition matters for treatment. Most conditions in FRH arise from the "faint or go mad" literary convention — witnessing horrifying things drives you mad, temporarily or forever. Some conditions have external physical causes (injury, poison, disease, spiritual attack) identifiable with a successful roll against Psychology (Clinical) or Diagnosis. In a campaign where demonic possession is real, diagnosing the patient's problem as conventional madness and treating it accordingly may produce unexpected results.

Cure and Recovery

Buying Off Disadvantages

Permanent conditions acquired through Fright Checks or Derangement become disadvantages on the character sheet and reduce point value. They can be removed by spending earned character points as described under "Buying Off Disadvantages" in the GURPS Basic Set — with one important constraint:

Problems eliminated this way do not actually disappear until at least one month has passed in game time per point bought off. A character who spends 10 points to buy off Obsession [−10] will still suffer the Obsession for at least 10 game months from the triggering incident.

Therapy

Professional treatment accelerates recovery. The therapist needs Physician (Psychiatric) or Psychology (Clinical) at 12+.

Care LevelRoll FrequencyTherapy Roll Result
Outpatient (at least 2 hours/week)Once per game monthSuccess: +1 bonus point toward buying off condition. Critical success: +2. Failure: no effect. Critical failure: costs 1 point (worsens condition or lowers self-control number).
Full-time care (home or institution)Once per game week

Bonus points from therapy apply only to buying off the treated condition. If the patient suffers a Fright Check for a shock similar to the original trigger at any point during treatment, all unspent bonus points are immediately lost.

At the GM's discretion, certain non-standard methods — successful use of Mind Reading, Hypnotism, or entering the patient's dreams via the Dreaming skill — may give +1 or more on the therapy roll.

Snap Out Of It

In the field, immediate triage is possible. Time-honored approaches (brandy, smelling salts, a firm slap) give the victim +2 to any relevant recovery or self-control roll. A professional with Physician (Psychiatric) or Psychology (Clinical) can do better:

  • The attempt takes 30 seconds. Penalty for haste applies in combat or mortal danger.
  • Success: The victim can function normally for 1d minutes. The Fright Check results return at full strength afterward, unless another successful roll is made.
  • Each point of success on follow-up rolls reduces total suffering time by 1d minutes.
  • These are cinematic rules. They do not mitigate permanent effects or halt physical effects such as seizures or heart attacks.

Institutions in 1923

Before TL6 psychiatric standards — which describes most available care in 1923 America outside major cities — therapy rolls in institutions suffer a −5 penalty even under the best conditions, which are rarely achieved. Some institutions are overcrowded, underfunded, or run by people with other priorities, imposing up to −5 additional. The GM sets the mechanical conditions of any specific institution.

Characters committed involuntarily face an additional hazard: they may be diagnosed with conditions they do not have and treated accordingly. A character with genuine knowledge of the supernatural, documented contact with impossible things, or abilities that cannot be explained is, from the institution's perspective, a compelling case study for schizophrenia, paranoid delusion, or hysteria.

Corruption

Some supernatural power is not free. Certain power sources — particular spellcasting methods, dark bargains, contact with malevolent entities, wounds from inherently Corrupt creatures — carry a Corruption cost. Corruption accumulates as a hidden tally the GM tracks in secret. The character may not know how corrupted they are becoming. The GM is encouraged to lie about it when asked: "No, you feel fine. Better than fine. Your moral clarity is unimpaired."

Corruption is separate from Stress and Derangement, but they interact: accruing Derangement also accrues Corruption (see below).

What Makes a Power Source Corrupt

The GM designates which power sources in the campaign carry Corruption. Not all supernatural capability is Corrupt — this is a campaign-level design decision. Sources that free the user from immediate energy costs by extracting a deferred price are the most common candidates: magic fueled by dark bargains, psionic abilities pushed beyond their normal limits, abilities granted by entities with their own agenda.

At character creation, every 10 points of Corruption is a disadvantage worth −1 character point.

Effects of Corruption

Corruption is visible in the bearer's aura. Saintly, divine, or inherently anti-Corrupt beings react to Corrupted individuals at −1 per full 25 points of Corruption.

Every week, anyone with even a single point of Corruption must roll against Will at −1 per full 10 points of Corruption. On a failure, Corruption converts to negative character points at −1 point per full 25 points of Corruption. Converting Corruption to disadvantage points does reduce the running Corruption total — the foulness reshapes the character into a briefly more stable condition.

The GM applies these negative points through any combination of:

  • Lowering self-control numbers on "evil" disadvantages (Sadism, Bloodlust)
  • Chipping away at "good" disadvantages (Honesty, Pacifism)
  • Imposing reaction and Influence roll penalties with good or pure individuals
  • Lowering combat skills against Corrupt entities
  • Imposing penalties on Fright Checks or Will rolls to resist possession
  • Adding quirks, causing afflictions, or imposing full disadvantages (Lecherousness; a demonic Patron with associated Duty)

Once a character has 125 or more points of Corruption, the GM may force conversion to disadvantage points regardless of the Will roll.

Cleansing Corruption

Three major approaches:

Meritorious Act
A major virtuous and selfless act — as adjudicated by whatever moral forces govern the campaign — cleanses 25 points of Corruption.
Penance
The character isolates themselves from ordinary life and devotes every waking moment to prayer or meditation. This requires a daily Meditation roll. At the GM's discretion, guidance from a suitable priest, mystic, or guru is also required. While committed, the penitent cleanses 4 points of Corruption per day. No cleansing is possible on any day a self-control roll is failed.
Divine Intervention and Other Methods
Bathing in a holy pool, drinking from waters of spiritual significance, or direct divine intervention may cleanse Corruption at GM discretion. Such methods typically require as much time and effort to arrange as equivalent penance would. Less virtuous alternatives exist — shifting the Corruption into an object or painting it into a portrait — but these carry severe secondary consequences and are almost certainly worse for someone else.
