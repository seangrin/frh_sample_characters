Sakuma Jiro

Former samurai retainer carrying spirit-sensitive rites and obligations outsiders will not understand - Medium, Blessed, Katana-19, and a moral grammar in which offense, boundary, impurity, and improper burial are the actual categories of investigation.

Powers

  • Medium [10]: Sakuma can perceive and communicate with spirits directly. In the context of improper burial, unanswered obligation, stained ground, and spirit presences that precede visible threat, this is not a dramatic spectacle - it is literacy. He reads the spirit world the way a navigator reads water.
  • Blessed 1 [10]: his spiritual alignment gives him a +1 reaction from deity's followers and a foundation for the exorcism and spirit-work that comprises his operational lane. It is not charisma - it is recognized standing in a specific moral and cosmological grammar.
  • Sense Spirit-24 (highest spell): his spirit-detection capability is exceptional even by magical standards. He can enter a space and know what is present, what is wrong, and what is owed before physical investigation confirms any of it.
  • Affect Spirits-21, Turn Spirit-22, Summon Spirit-17, Exorcism-16: the action side of his spirit-work - spirits can be moved, contained, addressed, dismissed. His response to spirit presence is not passive observation but active negotiation and resolution.
  • Katana-19 [24]: DX+4 at 24 points - among the highest weapon skills in the historical party. His sword is carried as a functional obligation, not decoration, and it can be deployed in scenarios where spirit work and physical intervention overlap.
  • Hidden Lore (Shinto Spirits)-17 and Theology-14: the intellectual and ritual foundations for everything else. He does not improvise responses to spirit problems - he applies a learned framework built over a lifetime of correct practice.

Required Secret Power

Secret (Spirit-sensitive former retainer carrying rites and obligations outsiders will not understand) [-20]: this is Sakuma's named secret. It covers his Medium capability, his spirit-literacy, his Blessed status, and the full body of shrine-and-boundary obligation that governs his behavior. Discovery does not just expose a power - it exposes a moral architecture that the people around him have no framework to evaluate.

  • Medium [10] - spirit perception and communication
  • Blessed 1 [10] - cosmological standing within Shinto observance
  • Spirit spell suite - Affect Spirits-21, Sense Spirit-24, Turn Spirit-22, Summon Spirit-17, Truthsayer-18, Sense Emotion-18, See Invisible-17

The secret is not merely the supernatural capability. It is the obligations those capabilities carry - the Vow, the Code of Honor, and the Sense of Duty that bind him to respond when the dead are improperly treated or a boundary is violated.

Required Secret Society

No named non-FRH Patron or secret-society affiliation appears in the source sheet.

Sakuma's non-FRH secret is individual rather than organizational: the Secret disadvantage covers his personal spiritual nature and obligations, not a society membership. He carries a moral and cosmological framework that no 1870 Western institution recognizes as a category, let alone an organization. His hidden-order lane, if it exists, would be the informal networks of practitioners and shrine keepers from whom he learned - but nothing of this kind is formalized as a Patron in the source.

This is likely by design: Sakuma belongs to a tradition, not an organization. The current page preserves the source as written.

FRH does not satisfy the secret-society requirement.

How Sakuma Uses His Powers

  • Sense Spirit-24 makes him the party's first-arrival scout for spirit presence. He can enter a space and determine what is present, what is offended, and what the probable nature of the disturbance is before anyone has begun physical investigation.
  • Affect Spirits-21 and Turn Spirit-22 let him act on what he finds: spirits can be addressed directly, moved, redirected, and dismissed within the bounds of his ritual competence. He does not merely detect - he responds.
  • Exorcism-16 with Hidden Lore (Shinto Spirits)-17 and Theology-14 means his exorcism work is theoretically grounded, not improvised. He applies learned procedure to spirit-world problems the way a physician applies diagnosis to physical ones.
  • Truthsayer-18 paired with Detect Lies-13 and his general sensitivity to emotional register means that in any scene involving witness testimony near spirit activity, he reads the emotional truth of what is said, not just the words.
  • His Vow (Do not leave shrine, dead, or boundary offense wholly unanswered when duty is clear) creates genuine operational constraints. A case that involves desecrated burial ground, an offended shrine, or improper handling of the dead activates an obligation he cannot walk away from. The party cannot always wait for him to finish what the situation requires of him.
  • Katana-19 means that in scenarios where spirit-world and physical danger overlap - possessions, dangerous threshold crossings, animated remains - he is capable of lethal physical response at high competence.

Investigative Role

  • Spirit-world detection and response - presences, offenses, boundary violations, improper dead
  • Ritual and exorcism specialist - the party's functional answer to spirit problems
  • Moral grammar anchor - frames cases through obligation, boundary, and owed response rather than evidence and motive alone
  • Physical intervention at high competence when the spirit-world and the physical world intersect violently

Why He Works

Sakuma proves that a spirit-sensitive investigator in FRH reads deepest when the supernatural lane is literacy rather than spectacle. He does not summon things for drama - he reads what is already present and responds according to an obligation structure the other party members will only understand later. In the historical party, he changes not only what clues are found but the moral grammar in which they are interpreted.

Character Overview

Sakuma Jiro comes out of a world that has already ended by the time most new companions meet him. He was formed by service, hierarchy, ritual, and a moral grammar in which one knelt, bowed, cut, buried, purified, and endured according to forms that once promised meaning as well as order. Modernity, displacement, and history have not erased those forms inside him. They have only made them lonelier. By 1870 he is a former retainer moving through a changing world with the grave discipline of a man who still knows what duty ought to look like even after the institutions that taught it have become unreliable.

That is what makes him more than a sword-bearing spiritualist. Sakuma reads cases through offense, boundary, impurity, neglected rite, improper burial, and obligation left undischarged. His supernatural life is not spectacle but literacy. He is the sort of man who can enter a place and understand that it is wrong before he can yet say how dangerous it will become. He knows that the dead remain present for reasons, that a shrine can be offended, that stain and sorrow have pattern, and that steel matters differently when it has been carried through the right kind of responsibility.

His virtues and wounds overlap. He is courteous, restrained, and capable of dry humanity, but he is also tired, rigid in places, and not fully at peace with the worlds he has lost or the people he could not serve well enough. Shame over failure, the temptation toward self-erasure through duty, and difficulty adapting to looser forms of life all belong to him. Those pressures keep him human. They ensure that his discipline feels costly rather than ornamental.

A new companion should understand Sakuma as a man still trying to be useful after service has stopped promising reward. He is not an exotic icon and not a glowing magical swordsman. He is a weary keeper of forms, a morally exact witness to impurity and owed response, and the sort of ally who can make even a practical case feel spiritually legible in ways everyone else will only understand later.
